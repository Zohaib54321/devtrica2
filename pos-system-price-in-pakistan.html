If you have asked three vendors for a POS system quote, you have probably received three wildly different numbers. One says Rs. 18,000. Another says Rs. 60,000 per year. A third quotes Rs. 350,000 and calls it "custom". None of them are necessarily lying — they are pricing three different things.

This article breaks down what actually goes into a POS price in Pakistan, so you can read a quote properly instead of picking the cheapest one and regretting it six months later.

Why two quotes for the same thing differ by 10x

The phrase "POS system" covers everything from a billing screen that prints receipts to a multi-branch platform with role-based access, supplier ledgers and cost-of-goods profit reporting. When a vendor quotes Rs. 18,000, they are almost always selling you a license to ready-made software that thousands of other shops use. When another quotes Rs. 300,000, they are quoting development time to build something around your specific workflow.

Neither is wrong. They suit different businesses. The mistake is comparing them as if they were the same product.

Three questions decide which side of that range you belong on:

  • Does your business work like everyone else's in your category? A general store that scans, bills and prints does. A distributor with area-wise salesmen, credit limits and delivery challans does not.
  • How much does being stuck cost you? Off-the-shelf software is cheap until you need one report it cannot produce. Then you are exporting to Excel every month forever.
  • How many people and locations will use it? Per-user or per-branch pricing turns a cheap monthly fee into a serious annual cost once you grow.

Software cost: the four common pricing models

1. One-time license (Rs. 15,000 – Rs. 80,000)

You pay once, install on one or two computers, and own that version. Common for small retail POS software sold locally. It is the cheapest entry point, but check two things: does the price include future updates, and what happens when your computer dies? Many one-time licenses are machine-locked and reinstalling costs extra.

2. Monthly or annual subscription (Rs. 1,500 – Rs. 15,000 per month)

Cloud POS, usually priced per outlet or per user. Attractive because the upfront cost is low, and you get updates and support included. The thing to calculate: over three years, a Rs. 6,000 per month subscription is Rs. 216,000. That is often more than having something built. Subscriptions make sense when you want zero maintenance responsibility, or when you genuinely need multi-branch cloud reporting.

3. Custom development (Rs. 120,000 – Rs. 600,000+)

A system built for your workflow, which you own outright. Priced on scope: number of modules, user roles, integrations, and whether it needs to run offline, on cloud, or both. This is the right choice when your business has rules that generic software refuses to bend around — batch-wise pricing, complex commission structures, unusual approval flows, or industry-specific compliance.

4. "Free" POS software

Free versions exist and some are genuinely useful for a single counter. The limits usually appear at exactly the point you need them: number of products, number of invoices per month, no multi-user access, no proper reports, or your data sitting on a server you do not control with no export option. Treat free as a trial, not a plan.

Hardware cost: what you actually need

Vendors often bundle hardware into the quote at a markup. It helps to know the parts separately:

  • Computer or POS terminal — a basic desktop or mini PC works fine for most shops. A dedicated touchscreen terminal costs considerably more and is only worth it in high-volume counters or restaurants.
  • Thermal receipt printer — the standard 80mm printer is the workhorse. Reliability matters more than price here; a cheap printer that jams at peak hour costs you more than it saved.
  • Barcode scanner — a simple USB laser scanner handles retail. 2D scanners cost more and are needed only if you scan QR codes.
  • Cash drawer — optional, connects through the printer.
  • Barcode label printer — only needed if you print your own labels, common in general stores and pharmacies.

A practical tip: buy hardware yourself from a local supplier rather than through the software vendor, unless the vendor is offering genuine support on it. You almost always pay less, and any standard USB device will work with well-built software.

The costs nobody mentions in the first meeting

These are where budgets break:

  • Data entry. Someone has to put 3,000 products with prices and opening stock into the system. If the vendor does not import from your Excel file, that is days of work you will pay for one way or another.
  • Training. Ask whether training is included, how many sessions, and whether it covers new staff you hire later.
  • Annual maintenance charges. Very common with one-time licenses — the software is yours, but support and updates are billed yearly at 15–25% of the license cost.
  • Customisation after delivery. Get the hourly or per-change rate in writing before you sign, not after.
  • Hosting. Cloud systems need a server. Sometimes it is included, sometimes it appears as a separate line later.
  • Data export. Ask directly: if I leave, can I take my data out, in what format, and at what cost? A vendor who hesitates here has told you something important.

How to judge a quote before you pay

Ask these six questions. The answers tell you more than the number at the bottom.

  1. What is explicitly not included? A good vendor answers this straight. A vague answer means change requests later.
  2. Who owns the data and the code? For custom work, get it in writing.
  3. What happens when the internet goes down? If billing stops, that is a business risk, not a technical detail.
  4. Can I see it running with my own data? A demo with sample data proves nothing. Ask them to load 20 of your real products.
  5. What is the response time for a problem at 8pm on a Saturday? Your busiest hours are their weekend.
  6. Can I add a module next year, and at what cost? Businesses grow. Systems should too.

The cheapest quote is rarely the cheapest system. The real cost is the cheapest quote plus the Excel workarounds, plus the staff hours spent fighting it, plus the migration you will pay for in two years when you outgrow it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest POS system in Pakistan?

Basic one-time-license retail POS software starts around Rs. 15,000–25,000 for a single counter. That price usually covers billing, a product list and simple reports. Anything involving multiple users, batch tracking, supplier ledgers or custom reports costs more.

Is a one-time POS license cheaper than a monthly subscription?

Over three or more years, usually yes — provided updates and support are included. A subscription at Rs. 6,000 per month costs over Rs. 200,000 in three years. Subscriptions win when you want zero maintenance responsibility or need cloud multi-branch reporting from day one.

Does POS software price include hardware?

Often not. Printer, scanner, cash drawer and the computer are usually quoted separately. Ask for an itemised quote, and consider buying standard hardware yourself — any well-built POS works with ordinary USB devices.

Why is custom POS development so much more expensive?

You are paying for development time, not a copy of existing software. In return you get a system shaped around your actual workflow, ownership of the code and data, and no per-user or per-branch fees as you grow.

How can I avoid hidden costs?

Ask for what is excluded, the annual maintenance charge, the rate for changes after delivery, whether data import and training are included, and how you would export your data if you ever left.
